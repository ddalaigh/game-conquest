The Caves

World one of Conquest. Deep under the mountain, where the dead still walk.

Behind you stands a wellspring of raw energy. It feeds you 25 energy every 10 seconds for nothing — and it is the thing the dead are walking towards. Lose it and the level ends.

Six creatures are bound to you. You do not place copies of them — you project their energy onto the field, which is why you can hold several at once, and why each one takes time to gather strength again.

Your six. Before each level you choose six creatures from everything you have bound, and at most two of them from Support. The sandbox ignores this and gives you the lot.

Projection caps. Four projections of any one creature at a time. Lose one or recall it and the slot returns immediately. Clearing a level for the first time widens one family's bond to five, for good.

For example:
  • Forge — Ember Forge and Ley Sprite, plus Storm Forge once bound. Energy and speed. The Bomb Rider comes from here too, but it rolls off down the lane rather than holding a square, and whatever it reaches is destroyed outright however much armour it has.
  • Dragon — Tiny Ember at 25 and Cinder Wisp at 50 for cheap early cover, the Bonfire that enemies walk over and burn on, Drakeling for steady single-target damage, and Stone Dragon once bound for a held flame across the three tiles ahead.
  • Guardian — Bulwark Tortoise to absorb, Thornback to make the chewing hurt, and Blower Tortoise once bound.
  • Winged — Phoenix for three lanes and piercing, Sling Roc to lob stones over everything at the back of the wave, and Ashen Phoenix once bound to raise your dead.
  • Support — Frost Moth, and Burn Moth once bound. Two uses per level between them.
The Bone King. Level 15 is a boss with 5,000 health. It never reaches the wellspring — it prowls the far side of the field, crossing to a new lane every 5 seconds, and sends its dead to do that for it: skeletons at first, then goblins and armoured skeletons once it drops below half. It roams constantly, but it only crushes every 20 seconds. When the time comes it stops dead, rears up, and its lane glows red. That is a wind-up, and when the slam lands it destroys every creature in that row — then the king crosses into a neighbouring lane and starts again. Recalling will not save anything, since a recalled projection is gone just the same: the glow tells you where not to spend. Put nothing new in a glowing lane, hold your energy for the two seconds it takes, and rebuild behind the king once he has moved on. An Ashen Phoenix is the only thing that answers a slam properly, raising one of the crushed where it fell. It will not walk into your wall and it cannot be blocked — the only way to end the level is to kill it.

Tunnels. A Digging Goblin burrows partway down its lane and surfaces two tiles closer to you. Nothing can touch it while it is under, and the hole stays open afterwards — every enemy behind it drops in and skips those two tiles too. A Bomb Rider rolled down that lane collapses the tunnel and buries whatever is inside it.

A Master Digger tunnels the same way, but its tunnel bends: the far end can surface in any lane, so the enemies that follow it in come out somewhere else entirely. Rolling a Bomb Rider down either end still brings it down.

Fire feeds fire. The Fireball Goblin cannot be burned at all — flame does it no harm whatever. Worse, it feeds: 80 damage of fire and the fireball swells, letting it burn through a fourth creature instead of three. Ice, stone, lightning and thorns are the only things that hurt it, so a lane defended entirely by dragons and wisps cannot stop one.

Sandbox. Everything free, nothing recharging, no projection caps, no way to lose, and enemies you drop wherever you like. It has its own fusion table with every item in unlimited supply — but anything you bind there stays in the sandbox. To use a creature in a real level you have to bind it at the real table with an item you actually earned.

The fusion table. Every level you clear for the first time yields an item. Between levels, open the fusion table, set a creature next to an item, and a matching pair binds a new creature to you permanently. A Drakeling and a Stone bind the Stone Dragon.

Upgrading during a level. An Upgrade row appears under the tray as soon as a creature you can upgrade is on the field. Buy one and click that creature, and it becomes the stronger version on the spot — a Drakeling becomes a Twin Drakeling, then a Fourfold Drakeling. Upgraded forms count against the same projection cap, so upgrading buys quality, never quantity. Fused creatures are final — nothing upgrades off a Stone Dragon, Cold Drakeling, Burn Moth, Storm Forge or Blower Tortoise.

Energy boosts. Two drop per level — weak 50%, standard 35%, power 15%. They bank between levels, so a quiet level is worth saving from. Pick a boost, then click a creature: weak is a third strength, power is double.

Recall is free and returns the projection slot.

The Sky

World two. Above the mountain, where nothing walks and nothing stands.

Seventeen levels, and no ground in any of them: everything you bring either has wings or is riding something that does.

No ground. Your Winged and your Dragons hold a square on their own. Everything else — forges, tortoises, thorns, the Bonfire, the Bomb Rider — needs a Flatbird set down first and placed on top of it. The bird stays underneath and still counts against your ten. Give a Flatbird an energy boost of any tier and the whole flock comes up: for the rest of that level every flightless creature floats unaided. Flightless creatures are not weakened up here, but the bird is a slot and a cost, and that is the tax. A Bomb Rider or Speedy Bomb Roller has no square to sit on and nothing to roll along, so it needs the whole flock up before it can be used at all — that boost is the only way to field one in the Sky.

Open sky. Every level has squares with nothing under them at all, marked pale on the board. They hold nothing — not a creature, not a Flatbird — and everything flies straight over them. They cut your lanes into shapes, and later levels cut them badly.
  • Globs — the faction up here. They watched your dragons and grew wings, and they drift over anything that cannot meet them in the air. A wall a glob can float past is furniture. Shielded at 200, armoured at 400.
  • Drift Globs pick a new lane every couple of seconds. A lane you answered is not answered for long, so breadth beats depth: Phoenixes, the Egyptian Cinder, the Storm Drake.
  • Brood Globs take 320 and then come apart into two fast Globlings. Killing one is half the job; keep something in reserve for what falls out.
  • Glob Airships shell the creature at the back of your lane — your forges, not your front line — and close half a tile with every shell.
  • Stormheads are weather. Ice, stone, lightning and thorns pass straight through them and do nothing at all. Only flame puts one out, which is the exact inverse of the Fireball Goblin.
The Thunderhead. Sky 17 is a boss with 6,000 health. It hangs at the far side and never closes. Where the Bone King crushes a lane, the Thunderhead rains out a column — and it picks the column you have built up most. When a column darkens you have under two seconds, and everything standing in it is already lost, recall or not. On a separate clock it also throws five bolts at five different creatures anywhere on the board, 60 damage each, with no wind-up and nothing to read — anything on 100 health dies to the second one. Spread wide, never stack a column, and rebuild in the hole it leaves. It calls globs while it waits, and what it calls gets worse twice as it weakens.

What the Sky gives you. Sky 1, the Gale Wings. Sky 2 opens the creature-to-creature table. Sky 5 widens your bond with the flock to twelve Flatbirds. Sky 7, the Thunder Egg. Sky 10, the Cloud Key. Sky 13, the Windblower. Sky 15, the Bird Mask. Sky 17, the Storm Heart, off the Thunderhead itself. The levels between them give nothing but a harder board.

The creature table. Once Sky 2 opens it, a Flatbird can be folded permanently into something that could not fly: Flatbird and Boxing Flames bind the Sky Boxer, which burns, and so answers a Stormhead. Nothing else you bring up here escapes the bird — your forges and your walls ride one or they do not come. The Sky's items bind the rest: Flatbird and Cloud Key give the Long Bird, three Flatbirds grown into one body that lays itself across three squares at a time and overlaps its own kind freely; Drakeling and Thunder Egg give the Storm Drake; and Sling Roc and Storm Heart the Thunder Roc.

You are here because the Bone King fell, and you are carried by the bird he was keeping. The Caves open one level at a time and so does the Sky: clear the level you are on to reach the next.

The Deep Sea

World three. Below everything, where there is no ground and no air either.

Twenty levels now, and nothing at the bottom of them yet. There is no ground down here and nothing floats on its own — the whole world runs on bubbles.

The Bubble Dial. A blue crocodile, and it costs nothing. Set one on a bare square and it blows a single bubble out through its nose and swims off — the crocodile does not stay, does not fight and cannot be attacked, because by the time anything reaches the square it is already gone. What it leaves is the bubble, and the bubble stays for the rest of the level.

Everything goes inside a bubble. Where the Sky lets anything with wings hold its own square, the Deep lets nothing hold one. Every creature you own — forges, dragons, tortoises, phoenixes alike — has to be placed into a bubble that is already there. That is the tax of this world, and it is a heavier one than the Sky's: you pay a crocodile's recharge for every square you want to hold. A bubble is not used up by what sits in it, so when the creature inside dies the bubble is still there and you can fill it again for free.

Give an energy boost of any tier to a Bubble Dial — or to an empty bubble it left behind, the crocodile being no creature to wait around for one — and the whole sea takes a breath: for the rest of that level everything holds its square unaided and you can forget bubbles entirely. It is the same mercy the Flatbird's boost gives the flock, and down here it is worth far more.

Trenches. A level can cut squares out of the board that hold nothing at all — not a creature, not a bubble. They are marked dark, and everything swims straight over them.
The Drowned. The dead that sank instead of staying buried. They come up the way they went down, only a tenth tougher — shielded at 220, armoured at 440 — and the water has taught them nothing except how to keep walking.
The Bubble Dancer. Something down here watched the crocodile work and learned the trick wrong on purpose. It takes the middle lane every time, and it dances the moment it is on the board — at the water's edge, out of easy reach — and the dance ends in a column of black-rimmed bubbles the dead drink like armour, 30 health apiece, first come, first served. Its bubbles answer to it, not to you.


This world is still being dug. Something lives in it now — twenty levels of it — but there is no boss at the bottom yet, no items of its own and no fusions.
